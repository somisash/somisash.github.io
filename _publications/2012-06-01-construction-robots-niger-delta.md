This presentation argues that construction robots can provide a lasting solution to infrastructure gaps, especially basic housing, by automating construction from excavation to finishing. It contrasts the high level of automation in the automobile industry with the low automation of construction, then proposes focusing first on single-storey bungalows, decomposing the entire build into process algorithms, and converting those into robot-executable code coordinated via CAD/CAM. Using the Niger Delta as a motivating case, it shows how a suite of robots—including a prototype hydraulic excavator called Robot Tirzah—could mass-produce compact, durable homes, accelerate post-disaster rebuilding, improve worker safety, and reduce costs while laying the groundwork for robotized roads, bridges, and other infrastructure.
