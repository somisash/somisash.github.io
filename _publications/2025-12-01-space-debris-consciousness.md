Space Debris Consciousness argues that the most serious constraint on space sustainability is not only technical limits but a lack of shared awareness about the debris environment. It traces how policy, commercial incentives, and everyday language all normalize debris as background “space junk” rather than as the visible record of specific choices. The essay weaves together case studies, public communication, and personal reflection to show how narratives about progress, deterrence, and convenience shape what risks we are willing to accept in orbit. It also sketches what a more conscious debris culture could look like, including new metaphors, rituals, and accountability practices that make the long-term consequences of orbital decisions harder to ignore.
